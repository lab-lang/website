Every durable action resolves through a typed contract naming the capability
required to dispatch it and how it treats each operand. This is what lets
the checker reason about physical ownership without knowing anything about a
specific instrument. `std.lab.plasmid_actions` defines seventeen of them.

| Phrase | Capability | Operands | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `capture image of <plate>` | `plate_imaging` | `plate` borrow | `Image` |
| `synthesize <design>` | `dna_synthesis` | `design` copy | `List<Fragment>` |
| `assemble <fragments>` | `dna_assembly` | `fragments` take | `Material<Construct>` |
| `provision <strain>` | `inventory` | `strain` copy | `Material<Strain>` |
| `transform <construct> into <cells>` | `chemical_transformation` | `construct` take, `cells` take | `Material<Culture>` |
| `recover <culture> for <duration>` | `culture_incubation` | `culture` take | `Material<Culture>` |
| `dilute <culture>` | `liquid_handling` | `culture` take | `Material<Culture>` |
| `plate <culture> on <antibiotic>` | `antibiotic_selection` | `culture` take, `antibiotic` copy | `Material<Plate>` |
| `pick <n> isolated colonies from <plate>` | `colony_picking` | `plate` borrow | `List<Material<Clone>>` |
| `screen <candidates> against <design>` | `clone_screening` | `candidates` take, `design` copy | `Screening` |
| `grow <clone> at <temp> for <duration>` | `culture_incubation` | `clone` take | `Material<Culture>` |
| `purify <culture>` | `plasmid_purification` | `culture` take | `Material<Plasmid>` |
| `split <material>` | `liquid_handling` | `material` take | retained + aliquot, same type |
| `sequence <aliquot>` | `sanger_sequencing` | `aliquot` take | `SequenceCheck` |
| `quantify <material>` | `dna_quantification` | `material` borrow | `Evidence` |
| `store <material> at <temp>` | `cold_storage` | `material` take | same material back |
| `dispose <material>` | `waste_handling` | any material, take | none |

A contract's `capability` is the name a backend advertises support for. It's
what lets the compiler ask "can this target even do this?" independent of
which specific instrument ends up performing it. `recover` and `grow` share
the `culture_incubation` capability; `dilute` and `split` share
`liquid_handling`. A backend that offers one capability implicitly offers
every contract that names it.

## Which of these actually reach LAIR today

Five of these seventeen contracts lower to LAIR: `provision`, `transform`,
`recover`, `dilute`, and `plate`. The rest, `capture`, `synthesize`,
`assemble`, `pick`, `screen`, `grow`, `purify`, `split`, `sequence`,
`quantify`, `store`, and `dispose`, parse, resolve, and type-check like any
other action, but `source_lowering.rs` has no case for them yet, so a
workflow that calls them cannot currently reach a backend.

`synthesize` and `assemble` deserve a specific note: a separate, higher-level
effect, `realize <design> from <dependencies>` (from `std.bio.build`, not
`std.lab.plasmid_actions`), *is* lowered, and the compiler's own
Workflow→Protocol dialect conversion later expands it into Protocol-level
synthesize and assemble operations internally, hardcoded to Golden Gate
assembly today. So the *capability* those two contracts represent is
reachable end to end; the source-level `synthesize`/`assemble` phrases
themselves just aren't the path that gets there yet. See
[LAIR dialects](/docs/compiler/lair-dialects) for how that expansion works.

## Typed inventory constructors

`std.bio.inventory` provides the typed constructors these contracts' `copy`
operands reference: `part`, `backbone`, `restriction_enzyme`, `strain`, and
`antibiotic`. Each names an external identity without resolving its
availability, lot, or provenance; see
[Syntax reference](/docs/guide/syntax-reference) for how they're bound and
used.
